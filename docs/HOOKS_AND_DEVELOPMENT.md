# Hooks, development and musical identity

Research and model proposal, 21 October 2026. Complements [Musical expression](MUSICAL_EXPRESSION.md).

## Scope and evidence

These are contrasting case studies, not a single recipe for a hit. For named artists/composers, the works below were chosen to expose different compositional mechanisms. Popularity is affected by performance, words, production, familiarity and social context as well as notes. Describing a mechanism does not prove that it caused a song's success.

Sources include scores, artist archives, interviews with participants and music-theory research. Musical interpretations and proposed generator changes below are our analysis, distinct from statements about the recording process. This is a source-based study, not a claim to have conducted a new listening experiment or complete transcription. No melodies, lyrics, recordings or artist imitation presets are being added to the package.

## Repetition versus development

Our working distinction: repetition teaches an identity; development changes its function or context while retaining enough identity to recognise it. Repeating an unchanged hook over an evolving arrangement can be effective development at the arrangement level. Conversely, changing every note does not guarantee development: it can remove continuity.

Use four independently controllable operations:

1. **Establish:** present a compact idea clearly, repeat enough to learn it, give it an audible role and space.
2. **Vary:** preserve a rhythmic fingerprint or contour while changing an ending, pickup, register or articulation.
3. **Develop:** fragment, sequence, invert, augment or answer that material toward a harmonic/phrase destination.
4. **Recall:** restore the recognisable idea after a meaningful absence, with a chosen change in context.

These are design categories, not measured laws of catchiness. A dance loop, a two-voice invention and a dramatic suite need different balances. ABBA's melody/chord craft and production hooks, covered in the companion report, fit this framework without becoming a compulsory template.

## Why composer 2 output could feel staid

The version 2 arranger introduced useful form, harmony, participation and ending controls. Its local composition remained narrow:

- `ArrangementComposer.Parts.cs` selects one of four contour arrays, uses short fixed rhythm templates and adjusts pitches to each current chord/scale. It stores no motif identity or transformation history. A strong-beat chord snap can change an important interval unpredictably across returns.
- Bass mostly alternates root and fifth. Multiple layers using the same pattern do not automatically acquire independent musical identities.
- Answer notes are placed in preset slots; they do not generally react to another layer's actual phrase, articulation or density.
- Harmony assumes bar-sized events. Key/mode and tempo are global; metre supports three or four beats, without compound-beat semantics.
- The form is a fixed family of intro, eight-bar verses/choruses, bridge and outro. A probability that a layer participates is valuable, but is not a complete account of how it enters, responds, builds or leaves.

SoundFonts improve instrumental sound. They do not supply any of these compositional decisions. More channels can increase masking unless each has a purpose.

Composer 3 now addresses the fixed verse-harmony and four-contour limitations with selected-cycle section variants, stored two-bar motifs, independent melody seeds and controlled phrase development. Bass hooks, responding to actual lead rests, harmonic spans, alternative forms and instrument-specific articulation remain work to do. See [the current studio guide](STUDIO.md).

## Proposed model and implementation sequence

The following names describe proposed types, not APIs that already exist. Keep composition, arrangement, performance and rendering separate.

| Priority | Model addition | First useful increment | Existing issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `MotifDefinition` and `MotifInstance`: relative pitch, rhythm, accents, rests, identity, source instance and transformation | Generate several original two-bar hook candidates; audition and pin one; realise four phrases as statement, restatement, departure and conclusion; return to the pinned hook in later choruses. | [#8](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/8) |
| 2 | `EnsemblePhrasePlan`: role, foreground priority, rhythmic occupancy, response window and density budget | Write kick/bass/guitar together; make answers respond to actual lead rests; let a signature bass riff persist across chord changes with controlled adaptation. | [#19](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/19), [#18](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/18) |
| 3 | `HarmonicSpan`: beat-based duration, local key, function, target cadence and compatible motif notes | Add changes within bars and chords held across bars; preserve hook landmarks when reharmonising; use a prechorus to prepare a distinct chorus arrival. | [#9](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/9) |
| 4 | `FormPlan` and `SectionIntent`: explicit material references, narrative role, energy/tension, transition and return | Add optional prechorus, postchorus, breakdown and solo; choose loop, song or suite form. Keep four two-bar verse/chorus phrases as the default and reserve a complete outro within the duration ceiling. | [#20](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/20), [#21](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/21) |
| 5 | `PerformancePhrase`: stress, breath, articulation, timing curve and instrument constraints | Coherent accents and releases; guitar strum direction, bass note length, bowed phrase changes and vocal-like breathing. Apply timing deliberately rather than adding independent jitter. | [#16](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/16), [#10](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/10), [#11](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/11) |
| 6 | `GrooveDefinition` and texture automation | Represent beat groups/subdivision explicitly, including 6/8 and 12/8; add repeatable role-specific groove templates and gradual changes of texture/space. | [#17](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/17), [#24](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/24) |

Expose understandable musical choices: **keep the hook**, **develop the phrase**, **build toward the chorus**, **leave more space**. Keep low-level controls available in advanced settings. Avoid one global randomness knob: harmonic novelty, motif development, phrase expression and arrangement density have different jobs. The existing probability controls remain useful inside this larger plan; deliberate structural constraints should resolve conflicts such as all foreground layers being absent at the intended hook presentation.

For endings, extend the current fade/cadence/tag/stop choices with a repeated refrain plan, staged subtraction and optional final intensification before release. Do not assign probabilities from this small, selectively chosen repertoire; weights remain caller preferences. Retain the early-finish duration ceiling and audible ending, including release tails.

## How to tell whether it improved

Tracked in [#37](https://github.com/panoramicdata/PanoramicData.MusicGeneration/issues/37). Create original, reusable comparison scores: riff/answer swing ensemble, dance ostinato, restrained verse to anthem, two-voice invention, slow spacious development and a contrasting sectional suite. These are mechanism demonstrations, not soundalikes of the named works.

- Store motif IDs, transformations, harmonic destinations, section intentions and performed decisions alongside the score. Verify the transformations, cadences, occupancy and playable ranges directly, beyond an audio hash.
- Compare current composition, unchanged-hook repetition and controlled development with the same seed, bank and comparable loudness. Also audition melody alone, bass/drums and full ensemble to locate the source of an improvement.
- Use a small blinded listening review: recognition on return, singability where appropriate, groove, intentional contrast, continuity, emotional trajectory and ending satisfaction. Record preference and disagreement; no numerical heuristic is proof of a hit.
- Test multiple styles and seeds. A sparse ambient piece must not fail because it lacks a large chorus; a contrapuntal piece must not be graded by pop singalong criteria.
- Preserve replay of existing written scores. Increment the independent arranger version for new composition output; retain the legacy generator's version/golden fixtures. Version new score fields and migrations deliberately, and save chosen hooks/performance decisions so rendering never makes fresh compositional choices.

The highest-value next implementation is priority 1 followed by priority 2: a hook that survives and an ensemble that makes room for it. Harmonic sophistication, expressive timing and production then reinforce an identity the listener can already hear.
